If it has been a while since you saw a dentist — or you are simply starting with
a new one — knowing what to expect makes the appointment easier. Here is how a
typical first visit goes.

## Before you arrive

Bring or have ready:

- **Your insurance card** and a photo ID.
- **A list of medications**, including doses, and any allergies.
- **Your medical history** — conditions like diabetes, heart conditions, or
  pregnancy can affect dental care.
- **The names of any previous dentists** so records can be requested.

If you are anxious, it is worth telling the office when you book. Practices are
used to it, and a heads-up lets them adjust the pace.

## The paperwork and the history

You will complete a health history. This is not busywork — it changes what is
safe. Certain heart conditions, joint replacements, and medications affect
treatment planning, so answer fully and flag anything you are unsure about.

## The exam

The dentist or hygienist will look at your teeth, gums, and soft tissues. They
will check for decay, existing restorations, gum inflammation, and anything
unusual in the mouth. They may measure the gum pockets around each tooth with a
small probe, which is a normal part of assessing gum health.

## X-rays

X-rays are usually taken based on your risk and history, not on a rigid
schedule. They show what cannot be seen by eye: decay between teeth, bone
levels, the roots, and areas under the gum. If you are pregnant or concerned
about radiation, tell the team — they can explain the approach and often defer
non-essential images.

## The cleaning

A hygienist typically removes plaque and tartar, then polishes the teeth. If
gum disease is present, the plan may differ from a standard cleaning. Deeper
cleaning, if needed, is usually scheduled separately.

## The plan

At the end, you should get a clear summary: what was found, what — if anything —
is recommended, the order of treatment, and costs. Ask questions until it makes
sense. A good answer is plain English, not jargon.

## If it has been years

You are not the first person to walk in after a long gap, and you will not be
the last. The goal of a first visit is not judgment — it is a baseline. From
there, you and the dentist can build a plan that starts wherever you actually
are.
