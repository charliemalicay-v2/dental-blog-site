Both clear aligners and traditional braces move teeth into better alignment.
The differences are in how they do it, how they look, and how much discipline
each one asks of you.

## The core difference

**Braces** use brackets bonded to the teeth and a wire that is adjusted over
time. They are fixed in place, so the appliance works whether or not you
remember it.

**Clear aligners** are removable trays, changed on a schedule, that apply gentle
pressure to shift teeth. They are nearly invisible, but they only work while
they are in your mouth.

## Candidacy

Aligners handle many crowding, spacing, and mild-to-moderate bite issues well.
More complex movement — significant rotation, large gaps, or certain bite
corrections — may be better served by braces, or by aligners combined with
attachments and elastics. Candidacy is determined by an exam and imaging, not by
a quiz.

## Timeline

Treatment length depends on how far the teeth need to move, not on the appliance
brand. Simpler corrections can finish in months; complex cases run longer.
Aligners are changed roughly every one to two weeks, with check-ins along the
way. Braces are adjusted periodically, typically every several weeks.

## Comfort and daily life

- **Aligners** are removed for eating, drinking anything other than water, and
  brushing. Most people are advised to wear them around 20–22 hours a day.
- **Braces** stay on. You avoid certain hard or sticky foods to protect the
  hardware, and cleaning takes more care around the brackets.
- Both can cause temporary soreness after an adjustment or a new tray.

## Cost

Cost ranges overlap considerably. Braces and aligners are often priced similarly
for comparable cases, with the total driven by complexity and length rather than
by the appliance itself. Many practices offer financing. Insurance may cover
part of orthodontic treatment for children and, sometimes, adults — it depends
on the plan and medical necessity.

## The decisive factor

The most common reason aligner treatment fails is wear time. If you will not
consistently keep the trays in, braces may deliver a more reliable result. If
appearance and removability matter to you and the case is a good fit, aligners
are often a strong choice.

An exam with imaging is the only way to know which path suits your teeth.
