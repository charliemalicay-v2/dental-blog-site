When a tooth is lost, the decision usually comes down to two well-established
options: a dental implant or a dental bridge. Both restore function and
appearance. They solve the problem in fundamentally different ways.

## How each one works

**A dental implant** replaces the root. A titanium post is placed into the jaw
bone, allowed to fuse with the bone over a healing period, and then topped with
a crown. Because it is anchored in bone, it does not rely on neighboring teeth.

**A dental bridge** replaces the missing tooth by using the adjacent teeth as
supports. Those teeth are prepared (reduced) and a single unit — a false tooth
suspended between two crowns — is cemented over them.

## Side by side

| Consideration | Implant | Bridge |
|---|---|---|
| Effect on adjacent teeth | Leaves them untouched | Requires preparing them |
| Bone health | Stimulates the jaw bone | Does not replace the root |
| Typical lifespan | Often many years with care | Often 10–15 years, varies |
| Procedure | Surgical, staged healing | Usually two visits |
| Upfront cost | Generally higher | Generally lower |
| Maintenance | Brush, floss, routine care | Needs special cleaning under it |

## When a bridge can make sense

A bridge can be a good fit when the teeth on either side already need crowns,
when healing from surgery is not advisable, or when speed and upfront cost are
the priority. The trade-off is that healthy neighboring teeth become part of the
restoration.

## When an implant can make sense

An implant is often preferred when the adjacent teeth are healthy and you want
to keep them that way, when bone support is adequate (or can be rebuilt), and
when the goal is a long-term, maintainable solution. Bone grafting may be needed
first if the site has been empty for a long time.

## The questions worth asking

- Is there enough bone for an implant, and does that need grafting?
- What is the condition of the neighboring teeth?
- What is the full cost of each option, including the crown and any grafting?
- What does maintenance look like for each, at home and at the office?
- What is the realistic lifespan in my situation?

There is no universal winner. The right answer depends on your bone, your
neighboring teeth, your health history, and your priorities. That is a
conversation best had with an exam in front of you.
